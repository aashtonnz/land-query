# Reset proposal (T-32)

Status: **approved 6 Nov 2026, with the owner's revisions to Parts 1 and 2 (below), and applied.** The revisions: collect any lawful source and gate publishing by licence, rather than collecting only licensed data; compute any cheap measure; sale prices and listing details deferred rather than dropped; satellite and other inferences kept as labelled area context.

## Why

The repo describes the 96-domain design from the original Gemini conversation. Real data has now tested it
([live tile](../findings/2026-11-06-live-tile-test.md), [inverse queries](../findings/2026-11-06-inverse-queries.md),
[`SOURCES.md`](../../SOURCES.md)), and about a third of the design can't be sourced legitimately. Two load-bearing
assumptions failed: Shared-DP reuse is about 3.7 lots per plan, not 1:64, and the speed gate measured laptop seconds
instead of cost. Meanwhile, the narrow thing the tests proved, area queries over open data with provenance on every row, is
the part no competitor offers outside Auckland. The repo should describe that and nothing more.

## Part 1. Decision D-12: product scope

> **D-12: Land Query is an agent-facing, area-queryable parcel fact service.** Every value carries its source, fetch time,
> licence and fit-for-parcel flag, and is marked as published, derived or modelled (with method). Collect anything that is
> lawful to obtain and whose source terms allow automated use; serve only licence-cleared data to outside users. Facts and
> inferences are about land and buildings, never about people: no owner or director networks, no occupant profiling.
> Nothing invented. Compute any measure that is cheap to derive, each checked once against something independent.
> Scope starts with Kāpiti Coast and grows one council at a time.

Replaces the scope implied by D-1 to D-9. D-1 (an unopinionated, agent-facing spine) still holds and is restated
more narrowly. D-2 to D-9 are marked as superseded where they conflict (Part 3).

## Part 2. Schema: keep, defer, drop

Based on the sourceability audit in the [live tile findings](../findings/2026-11-06-live-tile-test.md#sourceability-audit-of-the-96-domain-schema-by-family).

**Keep (in v1, sourced live now):** cadastre, titles (type only), easements and covenants (existence only); LiDAR terrain,
slope and change; building outlines; three waters; flood hazard classes and recommended building levels; plan zones and
overlays; heritage, trees, ecology; noise corridors; wind and corrosion zones; tsunami zones; regional hazards (flagged
coarse); adjacency (derived from parcels).

**Defer (real, but needs licence checks, coverage work or effort; one task each when its turn comes):**
plan rule text; issued consents; power and fibre; geotech (NZGD, S-map); valuations (current only); historic aerials;
groundwater, water takes and LUC; Māori land and Pātaka Whenua (terms to check); lineage (derivable from LINZ); census and schools;
traffic counts.

**Defer until a lawful source exists:** sale prices and history; building interiors (bedrooms, cladding and the like)
from rating detail or listings. Licensed providers and listing sites forbid scraping, so these need an API or data deal.

**Area context only (labelled, never a parcel fact):** satellite EO (10–20 m pixels) and other coarse inferences.

**Drop:** owner and director networks (personal information; the Privacy Act and LINZ's personal-data licence);
plan-change submitters (names people); occupant signals (letterboxes, ONTs, shadow and insurer-quote signals); insurer
status, berm leases and body corporate data (not public); the valuation objection letter generator.

Computed measures: compute anything cheap from data in hand, each checked once against something independent before it
ships. Add to the Kāpiti compile: sun hours and winter shading, horizon and view angles, overland flow paths and
catchment, roof planes and solar potential, cut/fill for a level building platform, and an approximate wind exposure
category. Defer those needing data we lack: road noise (traffic counts), driveway swept paths (driveway shapes),
CPT-based liquefaction (ground test data) and stereo fill reconstruction from historic aerials.
